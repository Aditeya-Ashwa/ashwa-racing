## Who Is Motul?

Motul was founded in 1853, making it one of the oldest lubricant companies in operation anywhere in the world. The company is headquartered in Aubervilliers, just outside Paris, and operates across more than 90 countries. In India, Motul products are distributed through Atlantic Lubricants and Specialities Private Ltd, with a presence that spans automotive, industrial, and motorsport segments.

The brand's entry into motorsport started in the 1950s, and racing has been central to their engineering identity ever since. Motul's 300V — launched in 1971 as the world's first fully synthetic, ester-based lubricant — remains a reference product in motorsport applications. The Double Ester Technology they developed for the 300V range, formally introduced in 2004, gives their motorsport lubricants properties that conventional synthetics can't match: superior film strength under extreme pressure, better retention at high temperatures, and thermal stability across a wide operating range.

For a Formula Student team running a combustion prototype through dynamic events — acceleration, skidpad, autocross, endurance — these properties are directly relevant.

## What "Race-to-Road" Actually Means

Motul uses the phrase "race-to-road" to describe their engineering philosophy, and it's worth unpacking because it explains why their motorsport involvement isn't just branding.

The core idea is that motorsport is a development environment. Extreme operating temperatures, short warm-up windows, sustained high-rpm loads, and rapid thermal cycling all stress a lubricant in ways that a passenger car under normal use simply doesn't. When Motul validates a product in a Le Mans prototype or a WEC team's gearbox, they're collecting data on degradation, viscosity stability, and additive performance under conditions that would take years to replicate on the road.

At SIAT Expo 2026 in Pune, Motul demonstrated this direction clearly. Their showcase included OEM-validated lubricants developed in collaboration with Mercedes-Benz and Toyota Racing Development — both examples of technology that started in motorsport and moved into production vehicles. Dr. Julien Plet, Motul's Global Head of R&D, described the approach as maintaining parallel development tracks: advancing lubricants for current combustion powertrains while building the fluid technology platform needed for electric and hybrid vehicles.

That parallel development is directly relevant to what Ashwa Racing is doing this season, with an active combustion programme alongside EV development.

## The E-Gen and NGEN Programmes

Two product lines from Motul's current portfolio are worth noting in context of where Formula Student is heading.

**E-Gen** is Motul's fluid platform for hybrid and electric drivetrains, engineered specifically for thermal management of e-motors, battery assemblies, and power electronics. The challenge in an electrified drivetrain isn't lubrication in the traditional sense — it's heat. Battery performance degrades with temperature, and power electronics generate substantial heat at high load. E-Gen fluids are formulated to handle immersive cooling applications where the fluid is in direct contact with electrical components — a requirement that rules out conventional lubricants entirely.

**NGEN** is Motul's sustainable lubricant range, made using re-refined base oils. The 2022 launch of the first NGEN motorcycle lubricant used 65% recyclable base oil content and required 60% less energy to produce than a comparable conventional product. For a motorsport context where teams are increasingly required to justify their environmental footprint — Formula Student's sustainability scoring is a real category — having a lubricant partner working seriously on this front is useful beyond just performance.

## What It Means for the XX6C

On the combustion prototype, we run Motul's motorsport engine oil and transmission fluids through a full season cycle — from initial bench testing through to competition. The operating conditions for our engine are demanding relative to the lubricant's designed duty cycle: we're running higher-than-road sustained rpms during Endurance, with a thermal load that changes rapidly between dynamic events.

The specific value for us is in consistency. Oil degradation mid-event is a reliability risk. A lubricant that maintains its viscosity index and film strength across the temperature range our engine operates in is one less variable to manage when we're focused on driver consistency and tyre wear.

Motul India has also been a practical partner during the testing phase, providing technical input on fluid selection for our drivetrain configuration — not just product supply.
